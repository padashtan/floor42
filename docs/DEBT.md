Each open item is one line starting with `- [ ] D<n>`; a paid item becomes `- [x] D<n>` with the commit or date. Do not merge items to dodge the gate.

- [x] D23 After three.js's binding tables settled (the 47th change of decade) the JS heap and V8's code still seemed to grow (about 11 KB a change, measured under DevTools), and nothing proved it bounded. Measured again with no DevTools attached, through `performance.measureUserAgentSpecificMemory()` and the JS heap after a forced GC (`performance.memory`), over 600 changes of decade (a second run of 540 agrees): the heap after GC goes 29.0 MB at the start, 34.0 MB at the 80th change, 35.0 MB at the 300th, 36.1 MB at the 520th and 36.1 MB at the 600th (the last 80 changes: +0.03 MB). The growth is a slow staircase of V8's compiled code, not a leak: between snapshots at the 100th and the 540th change script objects are flat (objects 5.57 MB both times, arrays 4.00 to 3.99, closures 0.41, strings 1.72, canvases 342, buffers' data 8.18) while V8's `InstructionStream` grows by 1.2 MB (2,757 to 3,167 functions compiled, an amount a program of fixed size cannot pass), and the browser's own records stop at their caps (`PerformanceLongAnimationFrameTiming` 115 to 200 entries, the cap). The DevTools-only `NetworkResourcesData` records are gone without DevTools. Method: `FLOOR42_HEAP_ISOLATED=600 npm run e2e -- e2e/heapRun.e2e.ts -g "no DevTools"` (`FLOOR42_HEAP_SNAP=100` adds the two snapshots); the page is made cross-origin isolated for that run only by a Playwright route adding COOP and COEP `credentialless` to the document (the daemon sends neither). What this does not prove: that compiled code never grows again after 600 changes; a leak under about 5 KB a change in script objects would sit in the noise of the totals, while the snapshot sums are exact to the byte (2026-11-05, branch `debt/d23`).
